[size=6][b][color=#e8905a]Wraithguard Toolkit[/color][/b][/size]
[i]Sort your custom mods into your OpenMW load order - without ever touching your curated Modding-OpenMW.com list.[/i]
[center][youtube]yx7eFfxFLlU[/youtube][/center]
[line]
[b][color=#e8905a]The problem[/color][/b]
You built a carefully-tuned mod list from [url=https://modding-openmw.com/]Modding-OpenMW.com[/url] with umo + MOMW Configurator. Then you added a few mods of your own. They need to slot into the load order - but running a whole-list sorter like PLOX would scramble the curated MOMW order, which MOMW [b]explicitly warns against[/b].
[b][color=#e8905a]What it does[/color][/b]
Wraithguard Toolkit treats your existing [font=Courier New]openmw.cfg[/font] order as [b]frozen[/b] and only works out where [i]your[/i] additions belong - using the real mlox rules - then writes the result back as a corrected [font=Courier New]momw-customizations.toml[/font].
That's the durable fix: it [b]survives every future Configurator rebuild[/b], and your curated list is [b]never reordered[/b]. Only your mods move.
[b][color=#e8905a]Features[/color][/b]
• [b]Frozen curated order[/b] - sorts only your custom additions; the MOMW list is left exactly as it is.
• [b]Real mlox engine[/b] - ported from mlox and cross-checked against plox: [font=Courier New]*[/font]/[font=Courier New]?[/font] wildcards and the [font=Courier New]<VER>[/font] token, ordering that bridges over plugins you don't have, and [font=Courier New][Conflict]/[Requires]/[Note][/font] warnings that understand [font=Courier New]ALL/ANY/NOT/DESC[/font] plus the [font=Courier New][VER]/[SIZE]/[DESC][/font] functions.
• [b]plugin-order.yml aware[/b] - with your list name set, it tells curated-list plugins apart from your true additions and flags [color=#ffb454]redundant[/color], [color=#ffb454]orphan[/color], [color=#ffb454]needs-cleaning[/color], and base-order-drift issues.
• [b]Conflict detection, the xEdit way[/b] - a plugin → record-type → record tree and a [b]field-level diff[/b] carrying the two-axis conflict status modders know from xEdit, [b]colour-coded[/b] ([color=#7ee0a0]green = agree[/color], [color=#ffb454]amber = benign override[/color], [color=#ff6b6b]red = a losing edit[/color]). See which plugin wins, and why - then build a patch plugin from the fields you choose.
• [b]See the change, not just the bytes[/b] - compiled scripts are [b]disassembled[/b] rather than shown as base64; a conflicting mesh opens each mod's copy side by side in the [b]mesh viewer[/b] (with its block tree and a field editor) and a texture the [b]decoded image[/b] and a difference map; and a [b]DIAL/INFO[/b] record reads as [b]dialogue[/b] - the line an NPC says, to whom, and under what conditions.
• [b]Cell Preview[/b] - walk a cell in 3D and check it for conflicts without loading the game: terrain, objects, interiors, groundcover, sky and weather, MGE XE water, shadows and ambient occlusion. Click an object for its ORI readout (which plugins created and changed it, and where its mesh and textures come from), highlight everything one mod added, and Shift+click a door to go through it.
• [b]Merged Lands[/b] - merges the landscape edits a load order would otherwise silently discard (a function-by-function port of David Von Derau's tool), with a slope limiter, curvature-weighted resolution, per-layer [font=Courier New].mergedlands.toml[/font] control, seam repair and conflict images.
• [b]Cell map[/b] - a heatmap of which mods touch which cells, in-app or in your browser.
• [b]Resource (VFS) conflicts[/b] - the loose-file "Data" conflict view for your data paths, in load order.
• [b]Rule maker[/b] - author and test your own mlox rules in-app, from a conflict you found.
• [b]Health checks[/b] - master presence/order verification, a lint pass, remove/rename a master, save-file checks and timestamped backups.
• [b]tes3cmd frontend[/b] - clean plugins without leaving the app (Morrowind/Tribunal/Bloodmoon are never touched).
• [b]One-click mods-folder scan[/b] - point it at your custom mods folder and it builds the subset for you.
• [b]Drag-and-drop GUI + full CLI[/b] - reorder rows, with a live, color-coded log ([color=#7ee0a0]green = inserted/moved[/color], [color=#ffb454]orange = heads-up[/color], red = error).
• [b]Fully themeable[/b] - [color=#bd93f9]Dracula[/color], [color=#a6e22e]Monokai[/color], [color=#61afef]Atom One Dark[/color], [color=#fabd2f]Gruvbox[/color] built in, and you can import your own or any [b]base16[/b] scheme.
• [b]Opt out what you don't want[/b] - disable plugins/paths (multi-select); anything already in your cfg is removed durably via [font=Courier New]removeContent[/font]/[font=Courier New]removeData[/font].
• [b]Safe by default[/b] - dry-run preview, timestamped backups, and it can either emit a corrected TOML (recommended) or patch [font=Courier New]openmw.cfg[/font] directly.
• [b]Cross-platform[/b] - Windows, Linux (an AppImage that also runs on the Steam Deck) and macOS (Intel + Apple Silicon), prebuilt for each.
[b][color=#e8905a]New in 4.2[/color][/b]
• [b]A Rust backend[/b] - plugins, meshes and archives are read and written by greatness7's [font=Courier New]tes3[/font] crates, in process. tes3conv is no longer needed, and no JSON is written along the way. Repeat conflict scans and cell maps are near-instant.
• [b]Much faster where it counts[/b] - Merged Lands on a 4,000-cell landmass went from 54 s to under 7 s (and half the memory); a 2048px BC7 texture decodes in ~70 ms instead of ~12 s; the lint scan is about 10x faster. Each moved piece is tested to give exactly the output the Python did.
• [b]Cell Preview and the mesh viewer are Gardenfell's viewer[/b] (Robin Hjelte), brought into the app: the ORI inspector, groundcover, a cell picker with a world map and the mod-coverage map, mod highlighting, zoom to cursor and a frame-rate cap.
• [b]Water[/b] - MGE XE's water with shore surf, the Vivec and Molag Mar sewer waves, hue and opacity sliders, and waves that no longer repeat tile by tile.
• [b]Linux AppImage[/b] with WebKitGTK built in (Steam Deck ready), and a Windows [font=Courier New]-webview2.zip[/font] for PCs without WebView2.
[b][color=#e8905a]Requirements[/color][/b]
• OpenMW with a MOMW list installed via the [url=https://modding-openmw.com/guides/auto/]Automatic Install Guide[/url] (umo + MOMW Configurator).
• The prebuilt release for your platform needs nothing else. From source: Python 3.14+ with tkinter and a Rust toolchain for the [font=Courier New]native/[/font] module ([font=Courier New]pip install ./native[/font]); see the README.
• mlox rule files ([font=Courier New]mlox_base.txt[/font] / [font=Courier New]mlox_user.txt[/font]) - the [b]Update Rules...[/b] button downloads them.
• [i]Optional:[/i] MOMW's [font=Courier New]plugin-order.yml[/font], and PyYAML (a built-in parser is used if it's absent).
[b][color=#e8905a]How to use[/color][/b]
See the included [b]QUICKSTART[/b] for the 5-minute version. In short:
1. Point it at your [font=Courier New]openmw.cfg[/font] and add [font=Courier New]mlox_base.txt[/font] + [font=Courier New]mlox_user.txt[/font].
2. Build your subset: click [b]Scan...[/b] and pick your custom mods folder (or load your [font=Courier New]momw-customizations.toml[/font]).
3. Set your [b]list name[/b] (e.g. [font=Courier New]total-overhaul[/font]) and point [b]plugin-order.yml[/b] at MOMW's file.
4. Click [b]1. Sort[/b], review the panels and log, then [b]2. Export[/b] a corrected [font=Courier New]momw-customizations.toml[/font].
5. Drop that file next to your [font=Courier New]openmw.cfg[/font] and re-run MOMW Configurator. Done - your mods sort into place on every rebuild.
[b][color=#e8905a]What it will NOT do[/color][/b]
• Reorder your curated MOMW list - ever.
• Write anything until you tell it to (dry-run is on by default; backups are made first).
• Guarantee your customizations are conflict-free. It helps you [i]see[/i] and [i]place[/i] them - the rest is on you. Customizations are not supported by the MOMW team.

[b][color=#e8905a]abot's tes3cmd scripts (idea credited, no code used)[/color][/b]
• [b][font=Courier New]missing_pathgrids.pl[/font][/b] and [b][font=Courier New]cell_conflicts.pl[/font][/b] - © [b]abot[/b]. Published as [i]Missing Pathgrids[/i] and [i]Cell Conflicts[/i] on abot's own site, [url=https://abitoftaste.modlist.x10.mx/morrowind/index.php?option=downloads&catid=58&Itemid=50&-Morrowind-tools]"Morrowind is Home"[/url] (Downloads → Morrowind tools), alongside MMOG, MRS and abot's other tools.
abot is behind a great deal of what makes Morrowind still worth playing - Water Life, Silt Striders, the merged-object and resource-scanning tools that half the community's load orders depend on. These two [font=Courier New]tes3cmd --program-file[/font] scripts are small by comparison and easy to overlook, so: thank you.
[b]The files themselves carry no copyright line and no licence text.[/b] No licence granted means the author keeps all rights, which makes these the most restricted inputs in this project rather than the least. So we treated them as [i]read-only inspiration[/i]: [b]no line of either script is in this tool[/b], and both of our implementations were written from scratch in Python against plugin binaries. What we took is the diagnostic idea, which copyright does not cover.
• [i]Missing Pathgrids[/i] - the idea: an interior cell with no [font=Courier New]PGRD[/font] record is a bug, because NPCs cannot pathfind there. Our [font=Courier New][NO PATHGRID][/font] check deliberately [i]diverges[/i]: the original only considers plugins earlier in the load order and so reports false positives, whereas ours accepts a pathgrid contributed by [b]any[/b] plugin.
• [i]Cell Conflicts[/i] - the idea: "show me every mod touching the same cells as this one." That became the [b]Focus on mod[/b] filter in our cell map (whose implementation follows [i]modmapper[/i], MIT).
abot - if you would rather we credit this differently, drop the mention, or not reference your scripts at all, say the word and it is done.

[b][color=#e8905a]Our own NIF reader (retired), and why it was written rather than imported[/color][/b]
[i]Retired: meshes are now read by greatness7's [font=Courier New]tes3::nif[/font] (see [b]tes3[/b] above). This section and [font=Courier New]NIF_PROVENANCE.md[/font] are kept as the record of the reader that was.[/i]
Until 4.2.0, [font=Courier New]wraithguard/nif/[/font] read Morrowind meshes with our own code. That was a licence decision, taken deliberately and recorded here so it is not revisited by accident:
• [b]pyFFI[/b] - LGPL. The obvious Python choice. This tool ships as a PyInstaller onefile binary, and statically bundling an LGPL library carries a relinking obligation that does not fit that distribution.
• [b]nifly[/b] - GPL-3.0. [b]io_scene_mw[/b] (Greatness7's Morrowind Blender plugin) - GPL-3.0, Python, and scoped to exactly this game, which makes it the most tempting option by far. [b]NifSkope[/b] - GPL.
• [b]nif.xml[/b] (the NifTools format description) - in a GPL-3.0 repository whose own licence status is [url=https://github.com/niftools/nifxml/issues/86]disputed upstream[/url]. An unresolved licence is worse than one that clearly says no, so it was not used as a source either.
• [b]niflib[/b] - [b]BSD-3[/b], and therefore the permissively-licensed reference to consult if a layout ever needs checking against an implementation.
[b][color=#e8905a]Greatness7 relicensed the [font=Courier New]es3[/font] library so this project could use it[/color][/b]
On 28 July 2026, [b]Greatness7[/b] - author of the Morrowind Blender Plugin and of [font=Courier New]Greatness7/tes3[/font] - offered to relicense the NIF library inside [font=Courier New]io_scene_mw[/font], and then did it: [url=https://github.com/Greatness7/io_scene_mw/commit/cbe18b558299e14ecd959183e3cf9ea096fe95df][font=Courier New]cbe18b5[/font][/url] adds an MIT [font=Courier New]LICENSE[/font] to [font=Courier New]lib/es3/[/font]. [font=Courier New]Greatness7/tes3[/font] was already MIT.
That was an unprompted act of generosity toward a project that had spent months carefully working around his code, and it is worth naming plainly. The relicensed library is [font=Courier New]lib/es3/[/font] [b]only[/b]; the rest of [font=Courier New]io_scene_mw[/font] remains GPL-3.0 because Blender requires plugins to be, and this project respects that line. See [font=Courier New]NIF_PROVENANCE.md[/font] for the exact boundary.
Within an hour of reading [font=Courier New]tes3[/font], the cross-check had confirmed this project's hardest-won layout - the typed bounding box - and found a gap it could not have found alone: [font=Courier New]NiUnionBV[/font], a bound type no file in either corpus carries, which this reader would have refused. A second implementation sees what a corpus cannot.
[b]How each field layout was actually derived - and what was deliberately not read to derive it - is recorded in [font=Courier New]NIF_PROVENANCE.md[/font].[/b] That document is the companion to this one: this section says [i]why[/i] the reader was ours, and that one says [i]where every fact in it came from[/i], with the worked derivations so they can be re-run rather than taken on trust.
Going GPL-3.0 was considered seriously: it would unlock io_scene_mw, pyFFI, nifly and - the bigger prize - [b]OpenMW[/b], whose NIF loading, texture handling and [font=Courier New]openmw.cfg[/font] semantics this tool models from the outside. The project stays [b]MIT[/b] for now, so none of those were read for the reader's field layouts. They came from the publicly documented format, checked against real meshes with [font=Courier New]tools/check_nif_layouts.py[/font] (retired along with the reader).
[b][color=#e8905a]three.js - bundled, not merely referenced[/color][/b]
[font=Courier New]wraithguard/viz/assets/three.cjs[/font] is [b]three.js r186[/b] (© three.js authors), MIT licensed, with its licence text beside it as [font=Courier New]three-LICENSE.txt[/font] (and in [font=Courier New]License/Three.js[/font]). It is used by one page: the [b]texture comparison[/b] ([font=Courier New]wraithguard/images/viewer.py[/font]) - the WebGL wipe between two textures and the lit-material preview, where dragging moves the light. The mesh viewer and Cell Preview that used to draw with it are the viewer shell's now (see Gardenfell above) and need none of it.
It is a [b]CommonJS[/b] file, which looks like an odd choice until the constraint is stated: modern three.js ships ESM only, split across [font=Courier New]three.module.js[/font] and [font=Courier New]three.core.js[/font], and [b]ES module scripts do not load from [font=Courier New]file://[/font][/b] - the origin is [font=Courier New]null[/font] and the CORS check fails. The comparison page is served over loopback ([font=Courier New]127.0.0.1[/font]) in the app, but it can also be saved and opened from disk, so it has to work from [font=Courier New]file://[/font] too. A single CommonJS file with no [font=Courier New]require()[/font] of its own runs as a classic script behind a three-line shim, which works either way.
Through r185 three.js shipped exactly such a file as [font=Courier New]build/three.cjs[/font] and we vendored it unmodified. [b]r186 removed it[/b] - [font=Courier New]build/three.cjs[/font] is now a stub that [font=Courier New]require()[/font]s the ESM module - so the vendored file is no longer upstream's own build. It is built by [font=Courier New]tools/build_three_cjs.py[/font], which concatenates upstream's unmodified [font=Courier New]three.module.js[/font] graph into one [font=Courier New]module.exports[/font] with esbuild: packaging only, no minify and no source transform. The provenance is therefore a command anyone can rerun, not a binary to take on trust.
[b][color=#e8905a]Archives and textures, and why they are ours[/color][/b]
Archives went the same way as meshes. Since 4.2.0 they are read by [font=Courier New]tes3::bsa[/font] (through [font=Courier New]wraithguard_native.Archive[/font]); before that, [font=Courier New]wraithguard/nif/bsa.py[/font] read them with our own code, for the reasons that follow. [b]bethesda-structs[/b] (MIT, Stephen Bunn) via [b]BSAFileExtractor[/b] (MIT, Pierre GAMBIER) would have been licence-compatible, so this was an engineering call rather than a legal one: it pulls in [font=Courier New]construct[/font], [font=Courier New]multidict[/font], [font=Courier New]attrs[/font] and [font=Courier New]lz4[/font] - the last with a compiled extension - ships a 49 MB tree covering Fallout and Skyrim record formats this project will never touch, and every archive in its own test suite is the [i]post-Morrowind[/i] BSA format, which shares an extension with Morrowind's and nothing else. Morrowind's layout is a header and three tables. Neither project was read for the format; ours was implemented from the public description and checked against a shipped archive with [font=Courier New]tools/check_bsa.py[/font], which still checks the crate-based reader today.
[font=Courier New]wraithguard/images/[/font] is ours for the same reasons and by the same method. Since 4.2.0 its per-pixel decoders (DXT1-5, BC4, BC5, BC7, uncompressed DDS, Targa) run in Rust ([font=Courier New]native/src/img.rs[/font]), a step-for-step translation of our Python decoders, kept pixel-identical to them by [font=Courier New]tests/test_images_native_parity.py[/font]; the history below is of the decoders themselves. Pillow would decode these textures, but it is a large binary dependency in a PyInstaller onefile build. It was used instead as an [b]oracle[/b]: the corpus textures decode byte-for-byte identically to it, BC7 matches on 19,380 random blocks across every mode and partition, and every PNG we write reads back through it unchanged. Used but not read, exactly as with NifSkope.
[b][font=Courier New]pydds[/font] was evaluated for BC7 and rejected on licence.[/b] It is the closest technical fit - DDS decompression bindings including BC7, which is precisely what was wanted - and it is [b]GPLv3-or-later[/b], which would relicense this entire project. That decision needed no technical argument at all. Two further facts made it moot anyway: it [i]depends on[/i] Pillow rather than replacing it, so adopting it would have added a dependency rather than removed one; and it is a compiled extension at version 0.0.8, marked alpha.
[font=Courier New]quicktex[/font] (Apache-2.0) would have been licence-compatible and was the option if a hand-written BC7 decoder ever proved too slow. It was not needed: ours matches an independent implementation exactly, and moving it to Rust took a 2048px BC7 texture from about 12 s to about 70 ms.
The BC7 tables come from the [b]published format specification[/b] - Khronos's OpenGL BPTC specification and Microsoft's Direct3D 11 documentation - not from any implementation. [font=Courier New]NIF_PROVENANCE.md[/font] records how that was verified, and why transcribing six hundred numbers needed a cross-check rather than a unit test.
[b][color=#e8905a]Referenced for formats & behavior (GPL - no source copied)[/color][/b]
We read these projects to understand file formats and expected behavior. [b]No GPL source was copied into this tool's MIT code[/b], so no copyleft obligations attach to it (the one GPL part, the cell viewer's page, is its own GPL-2.0 silo - see [i]The cell viewer page (GPL-2.0)[/i]); the credit is one of gratitude and correctness.
• [b]OpenMW[/b] - GPLv3. The engine that makes modern Morrowind modding possible. Referenced for [font=Courier New]openmw.cfg[/font] semantics, the [font=Courier New].omwaddon[/font]/[font=Courier New].omwscripts[/font] Lua formats, and VFS ([font=Courier New]data=[/font]) resolution rules. Also read for the [i]math[/i] two animation controllers apply -- [font=Courier New]NiUVController[/font]'s offset/tiling and [font=Courier New]NiGeomMorpherController[/font]'s [font=Courier New]base + Σ weightᵢ·deltaᵢ[/font] vertex blend ([font=Courier New]components/nifosg/controller.cpp[/font]) -- which is format behaviour, not code: our cell-viewer playback is an independent implementation.
• [b]Mod Organizer 2[/b] - GPLv3. Referenced for the "Data" loose-file conflict concept behind our data-path (VFS) resource conflict checker.
• [b]MWSE[/b] - © NullCascade, Merzasphor, Greatness7 and contributors. [b]GPLv2.[/b] We read [font=Courier New]MWSE/OpCodes.h[/font] to [i]check[/i] our opcode table and found it agreed with MWEdit on all 533 opcodes they share. Because MWSE is copyleft and this tool is not, [b]no MWSE source was copied[/b]: the shipped table is built from MWEdit (MIT), our own corpus measurements, and [font=Courier New]customfunctions.dat[/font].
That last file needs saying precisely. [font=Courier New]customfunctions.dat[/font] is a [b]data file in MWEdit's own text format[/b], describing the MWSE / MW-Enhanced script functions so MWEdit can compile against them. It is installed by running the MWSE updater rather than being part of the MWSE source tree, and it is configuration for an MIT tool, not a copyleft header - so reading it does not bring GPLv2 obligations with it. It contributes [b]360 opcodes[/b] the base game has no equivalent for, which is what makes an MWSE-scripted mod disassemble instead of coming out as raw bytes.
It spells parameter types symbolically ([font=Courier New]Long | String[/font]) where [font=Courier New]Functions.dat[/font] uses hex flag words, and the mapping between the two was [b]derived, not copied[/b]: the two files describe 106 of the same functions, so correlating those pins each symbolic name to exactly one bit value. The result matches the [font=Courier New]FLAG_*[/font] constants already taken from MWEdit's MIT header, which is how we know the derivation is right.
Where the two files disagree - two renames ([font=Courier New]XDrop[/font]/[font=Courier New]XDropItem[/font], [font=Courier New]XEquip[/font]/[font=Courier New]XEquipItem[/font]) and 26 differing operand shapes - [b]the existing MWEdit-derived entry is kept[/b] and the disagreement printed rather than resolved silently. 25 of the 26 are the same call: MWEdit says [font=Courier New]String[/font] where customfunctions says [font=Courier New]Long | String[/font] for a filename or object id, and UESP's per-function pages document those parameters as strings, which settles it.
The 26th is a genuine error and is corrected: MWEdit gives [font=Courier New]XFileWriteFloat[/font] a single float operand with no filename, where customfunctions lists two and UESP documents [font=Courier New]xFileWriteFloat filename (string), value (float)[/font]. Corrections live in one small explicit table in the generator, each with its evidence.
• [b]MGE XE[/b] - GPL-2.0. Read alongside MWSE for the same opcode cross-check; no MGE XE source is in the MIT code. Its [b]shaders are ported into the cell viewer's page[/b], which is GPL-2.0 for exactly that reason - see [i]The cell viewer page (GPL-2.0)[/i] below.
• [b]Wrye Mash[/b] (Polemos fork) - © 2017-2021 Polemos, based on Yacoby (2011-2016), Melchor (2009-2011) and Wrye (2005-2009). [b]GPLv2-or-later.[/b] Wrye Mash popularised the [i]features[/i] of removing a master from a plugin and re-pointing one at a renamed file ("Change to.."), and we read its [font=Courier New]mosh.py[/font] ([font=Courier New]FileRefs.remap[/font] / [font=Courier New]remapObject[/font], and the masters editor's [font=Courier New]GetMaps[/font]) to confirm the behaviour a good implementation must have: to remove, drop the master, shift every later master's index down one, and drop references that pointed into it; to rename, swap only the header entry and its recorded size, leaving references alone. [b]No Wrye Mash source was copied.[/b] The index arithmetic itself is dictated by the TES3 file format - a reference packs its master index in the top byte of its [font=Courier New]FRMR[/font] word - and our [font=Courier New]wraithguard/esp/[/font] [font=Courier New]remove_master[/font] / [font=Courier New]rename_master[/font] are independent implementations over our own tes3-derived [font=Courier New]Reference[/font] model, sharing the same math our (public-domain) [font=Courier New]merge_to_master[/font] port already uses for master remapping. So no copyleft obligations attach; the credit is one of gratitude and correctness.

[b][color=#e8905a]Documentation referenced (no code copied)[/color][/b]
• [b][url=https://en.uesp.net/]UESP[/url] - [i]Morrowind Mod:Mod File Format[/i].[/b] CC-BY-SA. The community's reference for the TES3 binary layout, and the source of [font=Courier New]wraithguard/tes3fields/schema.py[/font]: 46 record types, their subrecords, whether each is required, its declared width, and the named members inside the struct ones. What is taken is [b]format fact[/b] - a [font=Courier New]NPDT[/font] is 12 or 52 bytes; its first two are a uint16 Level - which describes Bethesda's file format rather than anyone's prose about it, and the generated module carries the attribution.
The schema is what lets the diff window say [i]what a field is[/i] rather than only what its value was, and what backs the [b]Format reference[/b] view beside a record diff. Nothing is guessed: where the tables are ambiguous the schema says so (a field with two documented layouts carries neither), and 56 of the parsed layouts are checked against the byte counts the same tables declare - all 56 agree, which is how we know the parse is right.
